# About the documents

The working documents use specification numbers and short names, so here is how they fit.

The flow is: public sources, into `pipelines/<source>/`, into `data/lake/` (Parquet, read with DuckDB), then `pipelines/derived/publish_series` copies a serving set to Postgres, which `web/` (the data site), `site/` (the landing page) and `apps/dashboard/` (Streamlit) read. Pipelines write the lake and never draw; the sites read the serving layer and never call a source API. The lake is the source of truth and the serving copy is derived from it, with a provenance log.

`DEVELOPMENT.md` is the conventions: the golden rules, the stack, the schemas, the commands, the code-review procedure, the chart house style and the credential rules. `CHANGELOG.md` is the build log, specification by specification, with what was verified live against each API and what broke. `specs/00_INDEX.md` indexes the specifications, which are numbered in build order: 00 is project setup and conventions; 01 to 12 are one data source each (EIA by region, EU gas storage, EU gas flows, EU power, US gas and power, OPEC, refining margins, weather, news, macro and FX, vessels, flights); 13 is orchestration and tests; 14 the analytics layer; 15 to 17 sanctions, a news supplement and a sentiment overlay; 18 to 27 the trading analytics layer (curve structure, physical fundamentals, refining econometrics, systematic signals, risk, macro, volatility, freight, gas analytics, the journal); 28 a geopolitical-risk module adapted from an external design, with 28.00 to 28.11 as its chapters; 29 to 31 social sentiment and EIA price coverage; 32 and 33 the Postgres serving layer and the Next.js front end. `specs/00_TRADING_CONVENTIONS.md` and `specs/TRADING_LAYER_RUNBOOK.md` hold units, benchmarks and procedures for the analytics layer.

Each `pipelines/<source>/NOTES.md` records what was checked live against that source's API and its terms. `infra/README.md` and `infra/DISASTER_RECOVERY.md` cover scheduling and recovery, `artifacts.md` how the published exhibit pages and Office exports are rebuilt, `weather-module-spec.md` is a draft specification for the weather module, and `NOTICE` credits adapted code.

Every value in the lake is in one of four shapes, defined in `core/schemas.py` and validated on write: `ts_long`, one number for one series at one time with its unit, geography, frequency and revision date; `flows`, a quantity moving from one place to another with a mode; `events`, a tagged news or policy event; and `vessel_positions`, an AIS position report.

The README charts in `docs/img/` are drawn with matplotlib by `apps/exhibits/readme_charts.py` from the lake. The dashboards draw their own charts with Plotly in the browser using the theme in `core/theme.py`, and `apps/artifacts/` publishes standalone exhibit pages from the same lake. `data/` is rebuilt by the pipelines and is not in git; most sources need a free API key, listed in `.env.example`.
